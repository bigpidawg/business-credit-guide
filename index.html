01. The Personal Audit: The Foundation of Corporate Wealth

The transition from personal credit repair to business funding is the most critical moment in a client's financial journey. As an agent, your first responsibility is to act as a gatekeeper. Business credit is not a replacement for bad personal credit; it is a multiplier for good personal credit.

Understanding the "Bridge" Strategy

Lenders are fundamentally risk-averse. When a business is young (under 2 years old), it has no "character" in the eyes of the bank. Therefore, the bank uses the personal credit score of the owner as a proxy for the business's reliability. This is known as a Personal Guarantee (PG). If you move a client into the funding phase with a "thin" or "dirty" personal file, you aren't just risking a denial—you are potentially getting that business "blacklisted" by major institutions like Chase or Amex before they even get started.

The Agent's Hard Requirements

Before you even mention business funding to a client, you must verify the following in the Dominator Engine:

  • The 700 Club: A FICO 8 score of 720+ is the "Safe Zone." While 680 is technically possible, it often results in lower limits ($2k-$5k) which discourages the client. At 720+, we see $15k-$30k approvals.
  • Clean Slate Policy: Zero late payments in the last 24 months. Lenders see a recent late payment as a sign of current financial distress, making the business a "High Risk" bet.
  • The 9% Ceiling: If a client has $10,000 in personal limits and owes $2,000, they are at 20% utilization. This is too high. You must instruct them to pay their personal balances down to under 9% to "inflate" the score before the business applications hit.

02. Lender Compliance: Passing the Bot Filter

In the modern era, 90% of business credit applications are reviewed by an AI "Bot" before a human ever sees them. These bots are programmed to look for "Fraud Flags." If your client's business looks like it's being run out of a basement, the bot will auto-deny them regardless of their credit score.

The Professional Identity Matrix

To pass the bot filter, the business must appear as a "Brick and Mortar" entity with established roots. This is where most DIYers fail, and where you, as the agent, provide the most value.

The 5 Pillars of Compliance

1. The Address Trap: Never use a P.O. Box or a UPS Store address. Lenders have databases of every UPS Store in the country. If the application lists a "Suite" that is actually a mailbox, it’s an instant denial. You must ensure the client uses a Commercial Virtual Office with a unique suite number.

2. Digital Legitimacy: A business using a @gmail.com or @yahoo.com email address is seen as a hobby, not a corporation. The agent must verify the client has a domain-based email (e.g., info@clientbusiness.com). Similarly, a missing website is a major red flag. In 2026, if a business doesn't exist on Google, it doesn't exist to a lender.

3. The 411 Secret: Banks still use the "National 411 Directory" to verify business phone numbers. If the client’s business number isn't registered with ListYourself.net or a similar directory, the lender's verification software will return a "No Record Found" result, leading to a manual review—or a denial.

03. Tradeline Velocity: Building the Paydex Score

Just as personal credit has FICO, business credit has the Paydex Score (by Dun & Bradstreet). A Paydex score of 80 is the "Gold Standard." It essentially tells lenders that the business pays its bills on time—or even better, early.

The Tiered Ascension Model

You cannot jump straight to a $50,000 Chase card. You must "prime the pump" with Tier 1 and Tier 2 accounts to show the bureaus that the business can handle small lines of credit first.

Tier 1: Net-30 Foundations

These are vendors that give you 30 days to pay for an invoice. The goal isn't the products (office supplies); the goal is the Trade Reference. The agent should help the client open 5 of these. Pro Tip: Pay the invoice within 10 days. D&B rewards early payment with a higher score than just "on-time" payment.

Tier 2: The Retail Shift

Once Tier 1 accounts are reporting (usually 60-90 days), the agent moves to Tier 2. This includes "Store Cards" like Amazon Business or Lowe's. These accounts carry higher limits and start to build the business's "Thick File."

04. The Funding Wave: Strategic Capital Extraction

This is the most dangerous and exciting phase. The "Funding Wave" is a coordinated effort to apply for multiple high-limit cards in a single 24-hour window. Why? Because it takes 24-48 hours for a "Hard Inquiry" to show up across all bureaus. If you apply for 5 cards in 2 hours, the 5th bank doesn't yet see that you just applied at the other 4.

The 24-Hour Execution Plan

As the agent, you must sit with the client (digitally or in-person) and guide them through the applications in this exact sequence:

  • Target 1 (The Big Fish): Chase Business Ink. Chase is notoriously difficult and follows the "5/24 Rule" (they will deny you if you've opened 5 personal cards in 24 months). We hit them first while the report is "clean."
  • Target 2 (The Multiplier): American Express. Amex is unique—if you are approved for one card, they often don't do a hard pull for the second or third business card applied for on the same day. This is how we stack limits fast.
  • Target 3 (The Safety Net): Local Credit Unions. Credit unions are more relationship-based. If the big banks deny the client, the local CU will often look at the agent's "Business Plan" for the client and offer a manual approval.
